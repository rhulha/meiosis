What is Meiosis?

Meiosis is the type of cell division that produces gametes (sperm and egg cells). It reduces the chromosome number by half, ensuring offspring receive the correct number of chromosomes upon fertilization.

  1. Prophase I — Homologous chromosomes pair up; crossing over exchanges genetic material between non-sister chromatids.
  2. Metaphase I — Homologous pairs align at the cell equator.
  3. Anaphase I — Homologous pairs are pulled to opposite poles; sister chromatids stay joined.
  4. Telophase I — Two haploid cells form, each with duplicated chromosomes.
  5. Prophase II — Chromosomes condense again in both cells.
  6. Metaphase II — Individual chromosomes align at the metaphase plate.
  7. Anaphase II — Sister chromatids are pulled apart.
  8. Telophase II — Four haploid daughter cells (gametes) form with unique genetic combinations.
